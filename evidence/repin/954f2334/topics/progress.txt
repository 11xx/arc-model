Where a series of work stands, read on request

PROGRESS
  `arc progress` answers "where does this series of work stand?" for whoever
  asks. It is pulled, never pushed: a command prints it only when asked
  for it, and no status, check, catchup, advisory or hook carries a line of
  it. The
  journal's own history, read by name (`arc journal catchup` and
  `arc journal events`), lists item edits as the journal events they are.

  A series is a journal artifact of kind `progress`, written by
  `arc progress start <topic> --title T [--follows <plan>] [--item T]...`.
  It needs no plan; `--follows` records the one it follows. Its items are
  journal events on it, one per edit, each with its time; nothing is
  rewritten. Items have short ids (`i1`, `i2`, ...) that survive renames.
  Edits, each one call on `<series>`, named by filename or topic:
    add <title> [--before|--after <item>] [--from [<project>::]<file>] [--wave W] [--on REF]...
    rename <item> <title>                   move <item> --before|--after <item>
    drop <item> --reason R                  done <item> [--by <artifact|cp>] [--note N]
    reopen <item> [--note N]                link <item> [<project>::]<change>
    unlink <item>                           source <item> [<project>::]<file>
    unsource <item>                         waves add|rename|move|remove ...
    wave <item> <wave>                      unwave <item>
    depend <item> --on <ref>                undepend <item> --on <ref>
    wait <item> <discussion> --question Q   unwait <item>
    split <item> --into T --into T... --reason R [--wave W]
    transfer|defer <item> --to [<project>::]<target> [--wave W] --reason R
  Items take no generic correction; each edit has its reversal. `reopen`
  undoes `drop` and `done` alike, for any item; a reopened linked item reads
  its derived stage again. A rename is undone by another rename, a move by
  another move, a link by `unlink`, a wait by `unwait`. A split or transfer
  is final: `split` replaces an item with new parts, and `transfer` (aliased
  as `defer`) moves it to another local or cross-project series while
  preserving title, `--from` source, active change link, and matching wave
  (reverse a transfer by transferring back). A dropped, split, or transferred
  item stays visible and leaves the count.

  `done` is for an item no change carries; a linked item's stage is the
  ledger's. Optional `--by <target>` on `done` cites the resolving bare
  `conclusion` or `decision` filename of this journal, or a recorded
  checkpoint name of this repository's ledger.
  `source` (and `--from` on `add` or `start`) binds an item to its originating
  journal artifact (`todo`, `feature-request`, `plan`, or `handoff`), either a
  bare filename in this journal or `<project>::<file>` in another registered
  project; `unsource` removes it. The report derives whether that source is
  `open`, `consumed`, `archived`, `absent`, or `unknown` (unreadable foreign
  journal) and flags drift only when a `done` or `integrated` item's non-plan
  source filing remains live and `open`.
  `arc begin <slug> --item <series>:<item>` links when the change
  opens, checking the item before anything is written; `start` ends by
  naming that call. When that item carries a `--from` source that is still
  open and actionable, `begin` propagates it as `--from-journal` if none was
  passed, or refuses if an explicit `--from-journal` names a different
  artifact.
  On an unlinked `open` item, the report surfaces live advisory pre-change
  ownership (`claimed`, with `◈` in the terminal view) when either a live
  `arc journal lane open --item <series>:<item>` targets the item or an active
  `arc claim` holds its `--from` source artifact; the `claimed` count overlaps
  `open`. `link` names a change of this project, or
  `<project>::<change>` one of another registered project by slug or label;
  the link records that project's slug, and the item then reads that
  project's ledger exactly as a local link reads this one's, the row naming
  the project beside the change. An unregistered project, or a ledger of it
  that cannot be read, makes that item `unknown` and fails nothing. A link
  replaces an item's link only when that change closed without integrating,
  judged from the ledger holding it, and only while the link is still the
  one the command read.

  An item waits on a person through a journal question: pose it on a
  discussion (`arc journal question`), then tie it to the item with
  `wait <item> <discussion> --question <id>`. The discussion is a bare
  filename of this journal and must hold that question, still open for a
  person; both are checked before anything is written. The wait is derived,
  never cleared by hand: while the question is open the row carries it
  (file, id and heading) and the series counts it as waiting, and once the
  question is answered the row shows nothing of it. A wait leaves the stage
  as it is.

  Stages. Unlinked: `open`, `done`, `dropped`, `split`, `transferred`. Linked, as the
  change's own events in the ledger holding it record it: `opened`;
  `snapshotted` (a patchset is recorded); `verified` (at the newest
  patchset's head at least one gate has a recorded run and every gate's most
  recent run there is a pass; a run that was not admitted is passed over, and
  a failure recorded after a pass takes it back to `snapshotted`);
  `reviewed` (a verdict is recorded on the newest patchset, shown with the
  rounds that asked for changes); `integrated` (with its time and revision);
  `closed` (closed without integrating); `unknown` (no such change). A stage
  is what the ledger last recorded, never a live judgement, so it can lag
  the change but never run ahead of it; `arc status <change>` is the live
  reading. So work done outside the ledger shows only once it is recorded:
  a review round run outside arc shows when its verdict is recorded
  (`arc review <change> --verdict changes-requested --cause <cause>
  --on-behalf-of <reviewer>` per round), and an executor that commits
  without `arc snapshot` leaves its item at `opened`. The report also names who holds a live claim on the
  change, and, for a change in flight, the newest run the ledger records
  dispatched against it with no recorded end (`arc run dispatch`, `bind`,
  `end`): to whom (the represented subject, else the route), when, and
  whether a backend session is bound. That is the ledger's record of a
  dispatch, not a reading of whether the run is alive; no runner is asked.

  The share complete is a count: integrated and done items over the items that
  stand, printed as counts first and one percentage after. No estimate of
  time, no weighting. The report also says how many items were declared at
  the start and how many were added, dropped, split and transferred since. The waiting
  count overlaps the others: an item that waits is also counted under its
  stage.

  Waves, prerequisites and readiness. A series may declare ordered waves
  (`start --wave <id>[=<title>]`, `waves add|rename|move|remove`) and assign
  items (`add --wave`, `wave <item> <wave>`, `unwave <item>`; `split` inherits
  its item's wave unless given `--wave`). Waves are advisory by default unless
  declared `--gated` (or the series starts with `--gated-waves`): a gated wave
  blocks its open items until every standing item in earlier declared waves is
  `integrated` or `done`. Explicit prerequisites (`depend <item> --on <ref>`,
  `undepend`) accept `<item>`, `<series>:<item>`, or
  `<project>::<series>:<item>` and refuse cycles at write time; a split
  prerequisite expands to its standing parts, a transferred prerequisite follows
  `transferred_to` to its destination, a dropped prerequisite stays
  unsatisfied until removed, and an unreadable foreign journal degrades that
  prerequisite to `unknown` without failing the report. Each row derives
  `readiness` (`ready`, `claimed`, `blocked`, `waiting`, `in-flight`,
  `complete`, or `inactive`) and `blocked_by`. `arc progress [<series>] --ready`
  prints only currently ready items (excluding claimed, blocked and waiting
  items), and `arc watch <series> --until item-ready|wave-done [--wave W]`
  waits on the workspace feed until a ready item or completed wave appears.

  `arc progress` prints every series of this project that is not retired, in
  full, one block per series; `arc progress <series>` narrows to one and
  reads a retired series too. After the local blocks come any open journal
  questions that wait on a person.

  One report prints in three forms. The default is a view for a person. A
  series is its title, a bar of twenty cells shared in proportion between
  the complete, in-flight and remaining items, and one line per item: a
  mark, the id and title, then the stage and beside it the change, or the
  revision and age of the integration; a change of another project prints
  as `<project>::<change>`, and a dispatch as `dispatched to <subject>
  <age>`, `unbound` when no session is bound. The marks are `✓` complete,
  `○` open, a circle that fills from `opened` to `reviewed`, `×` dropped or
  closed, `÷` split and `?` unknown. An open item that waits reads
  `waiting` with a `?`; any other item that waits keeps its stage with
  `waiting` beside it. The question's heading takes a faint line under the
  title, and the bar counts the waiting items. The view names a series by its topic
  and gives a past time as an age; the title of an item past `open`
  continues on the next line when wider than the column before its stage,
  and nothing is cut. It is painted only when standard output is a terminal,
  `NO_COLOR` is unset or empty and `TERM` is not `dumb`; its words are the
  same unpainted. `--plain` prints every recorded fact as line-oriented text
  that is never painted, with filenames, change ids and full times, each
  series under a `==` header line. `--json` prints the versioned shape. A
  reader that acts on the report takes `--plain` or `--json`.

  A report
  reads one event log per journal it shows and reduces only the linked
  changes; finding a stage starts no Git process of its own. So what it
  reads follows its series, linked items and related series. Git runs to
  locate a project and open its ledger, and wherever reducing a linked
  change does, as when the ledger checks a reused base pass. Opening a
  ledger, which happens only for a project with a linked item, pays the
  store's shared cost once: its repository-wide records, the import batches
  among them, which grow with the ledger. Dispatches are read from those
  records. Each other project an item links into or a related series lives
  in opens its ledger at most once per report, and the registry is read
  once, only when an item or relation names another project. A ledger of
  this project that cannot be read fails the report, puts its project among
  `arc workspace progress`'s failures, and makes a related series
  unavailable.

  Related series. `arc progress relate <series> <project>::<series>` records,
  in this journal only, that a series relates to one in another registered
  project, named by slug or label as other cross-project journal references
  are; `unrelate` with the same arguments removes it here. The same call in
  the other project makes it mutual; until then the block names the relation
  directly under its header as `one-sided`. Each related series then prints
  after the local ones in compact form, in `--plain` under a `--` header
  line: its counts, last integration and in-flight items, read from that
  project's journal events and ledger, and the ledger of each project its
  items link into. A related journal that cannot be read prints one line and
  fails nothing. Relations share no items and roll up no percentage.

  `arc progress retire <series> --by <conclusion>` records the series as
  `consumed`, outcome `superseded`, naming the conclusion as what resolved
  it, and the journal's inventories show the conclusion as its successor.
  `--by` and `--follows` take a bare filename of this journal; a path,
  another journal's reference or a symbolic link is refused. A retired series
  leaves the default listing, stays readable by name, and refuses every
  `arc progress` edit. As for every consumed artifact, the journal's
  amendments still apply: `arc journal correct` replaces its recorded title,
  which the report then shows, and `arc journal retract` withdraws evidence
  or a position filed on it. `arc workspace progress` prints every registered
  project's active series in the same three forms, and says whether a
  relation is mutual from the series it read itself: absent only when the
  related series was not read by that command. `--json` prints
  `arc-progress/1` and `arc-workspace-progress/1`. `arc workspace report`
  and `arc workspace watch`, given `--progress`, carry the same series per
  project as `projects[].progress`, for a page that draws the workspace,
  each project read alone, so no relation's `mutual` is filled there; a
  series that cannot be read is a failed `progress` component there.
