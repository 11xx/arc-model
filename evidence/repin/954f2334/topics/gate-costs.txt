Cost diagnosis, levers and check timings

GATE COSTS
  `arc gates` in text and JSON and `arc gates over` diagnose command costs
  from receipts. Indicators can overlap; they suggest what to measure and do
  not establish a cause or alter gate acceptance. Time distributions use at
  most the latest 20 executed runs matching the current command and declared
  timeout, in the newest recorded environment. Medians use the lower middle.
  `gates over` uses the selected overrun's effective declarations, including
  gates declared only on its change, from any checkout. Without an overrun it
  uses the invoking checkout's declarations.
  Each measure reports its sample count. Missing fields are unmeasured:
  legacy duration_ms never substitutes for command, CPU, wait or preparation.

  Linux counts user-space instructions across inherited descendants with a
  holder-owned perf_event_open counter installed before exec and read after
  the tree exits. An inherited task-clock counter supplies total tree CPU,
  including auto-reaped children; waited user/system time stays separate.
  CPU masks are checked at entry and the unreaped leader's exit.
  /proc/stat busy CPU excludes idle and I/O wait; capacity is elapsed time
  times online logical CPUs. The holder samples across the command tree's
  lifetime. Arc also samples from invocation startup to gate spawn, subtracting
  its process and known child-tree CPU, and excludes its measured CPU during runs.
  Incomplete, multiplexed or refused perf counters are absent measurements.

  The busy-host rule excuses a command time over its budget only when every
  condition of the rule version its receipt names holds. Version 2, the
  current rule, requires, against the newest eligible reference:
  - at most 3% more user-space instructions, at a rate of at least 10% of
    the reference's instructions per tree user-CPU second; zero counts and
    lower rates are absent evidence.
  - at least 25% unrelated CPU use both before and during the gate. Before
    is verification startup to just before spawn, at least 100 ms, less
    arc's process CPU and every earlier tree it ran. During is host busy CPU
    less the gate tree and arc's other measured CPU.
  - a clock within reference.run_ms * (1 - reference_share) / (1 -
    other_share), with 25% slack: each run's capacity left by other work.
  - a tree clock covering the run's waited user and system CPU at a share
    at most 5% below the reference's own share. Wait also reports process
    teardown the clock never sees, about a tenth of the CPU of a command
    that starts many processes, so the share is compared with the
    reference's and never with a fixed bound.
  Version 1 holds the same thresholds without the coverage condition. A
  receipt carries its reference, rule version and thresholds; readers judge
  it by the thresholds of the version it names, which must match that
  version's exactly, and by the current budget, without replacing the
  reference or consulting live load. A threshold change takes a new rule
  version. Unattributed concurrent batches, a holder or leader with
  restricted CPUs, missing or incomplete counters or host readings, and
  under version 2 a missing tree clock on the run or its reference, get no
  excuse. Other arc invocations' parallel lanes qualify
  only through the before condition.

  A reference is a clean, unmoved, unexcused direct pass, outside --parallel
  batches and acceptance probes, of the same gate and declaration on the same known hostname,
  logical CPU count and CPU model. Its tree is this run's tree or its revision
  leads to this run's destination. Its clock must fit the budget in force.
  Cached receipts never supply references.
  Legitimate added work becomes its own reference after one clock-fitting pass
  on a quiet host. Lowering the budget retires an excuse whose reference
  stops fitting. Excused runs never become references, raise evidence or
  base-pass seeds, including after a raise; neither do runs recorded under
  a suspension (`arc guide gates`). Kernel instructions are
  uncounted. Known limit: added waiting on an already busy host can fit the
  measured delay allowance; a quiet run exposes it. What the counters cannot
  follow is in `arc guide change-gates`.

  Refusals include this change's per-change increment and maximum from its
  destination budget, including the first observed refusal. They name command
  time, effective budget and sources, excess, host,
  start load averages and online cores, CPU where recorded, and excluded wait
  and preparation. Missing observations stay missing. Load above the core count
  may indicate possible host contention; start load alone grants no excuse.

  Verify warns when the median of up to 20 matching command-time observations
  in this change exceeds 80% of its budget; legacy total durations are excluded.

  Budget shares use at most the latest 20 command-time observations for the
  current command and declared timeout, in the newest observed environment.
  Legacy duration_ms samples remain in the named historical distribution and
  never enter budget shares. A median above 80% is labelled in the report.

  `arc workspace gates` rows include declared total, landing median and p90,
  and landings, paid gate time, preparation, arc's own time and landing
  time in 7/30 days. They sort by 30-day measured gate time descending (the
  known lower bound when incomplete), then project name; unobserved
  projects follow readable ones.

  Landing costs use the latest 100 readable integrated changes, including
  asserted and legacy closures and each completed stack member separately.
  Each change pays the sum of run_ms of its executed, named integration gates
  through closure, including failures and reruns. Reuse, selection, base passes,
  attestation and not-admitted attempts pay nothing; publication is excluded.
  A stack's shared execution is charged only to the member recording it.
  Missing run_ms makes that change unmeasured; duration_ms is never substituted.
  Median (lower middle) and p90 (nearest rank) are known only when assigning
  every unmeasured cost zero or arbitrarily large values yields the same result;
  otherwise that statistic and any dependent budget judgment are unmeasured.
  The report names missing samples and the zero-execution share of all window
  landings. Empty history has no median or share.
  The 7/30-day windows use closure time and include every readable change
  landed within that period, charging its complete landing cost there.
  Paid totals are null if any such change is unmeasured or the sum
  overflows; measured_ms retains the known lower bound. Unreadable changes
  remain collection failures.
  Preparation paid is reported beside gate time for the same landings and
  windows, and for each gate over its runs, from prepare_ms on the same
  receipts by the same rules: a receipt without prepare_ms leaves its
  landing's preparation unmeasured, and a percentile is stated only when
  known. The landing budget judges gate time alone.

  Landing time is what a landing cost the person waiting: gate time,
  preparation and arc's own commands, summed per landing, window and
  period, and by `arc show` for an integrated change. Each event's stamp
  (`arc guide schemas`) gives its process's running time and the gate and
  preparation time it had recorded, as the two columns count it, on any
  change. arc's own time sums, over the processes that wrote the change's
  events from the shipped patchset's `patchset-added` through the
  integration event (the `arc stats --round-trip` window), running time
  less that gate time at the change's last event in each; no other ledger
  is read. Admission waiting (`wait_ms`) and publication or not-admitted
  gate runs stay in arc's own time; a process its concurrent gates outran
  counts zero. Time between commands is not counted. Work after a
  command's last event on the change is not seen, so each command's time
  is a lower bound; a command landing several changes counts in each. An
  event lacking a stamp field leaves its landing's own time and sum
  unmeasured. The sum is reported, never budgeted: it includes time a
  project cannot change, and the landing budget judges gate time alone.

  Total bounds the worst-case integration suite and is enforced.
  Landing declares typical executed cost and is advisory, reported against
  the latest-100 median with a doctor advice finding when exceeded.
  Declare landing = "2s" in [budget]; it needs no raise record and never
  changes acceptance. Its value comes from the invoking change's destination
  branch, or the current branch without a matching change, combined with the
  operator's landing declaration by minimum; change-head additions do not bind.
  A detached checkout without a matching destination reports it unobserved.
  A [budget] table enforces when it has no `landing` key, the empty table
  included, or when beside `landing` it sets `total`, `raise`, `raise_per`
  or `exclude` to other than its default; `landing` alone, or with those
  keys at their defaults such as an empty `exclude`, does not. An enforcing
  table in any layer of the effective suite makes every required
  integration gate need a budget, in every profile. Per-gate budgets
  without such a table require none of the others. The gates needing one include a gate the destination
  declares and a change's head removes, since the change still owes it. A
  removed gate that has a budget on the
  destination keeps it; one that has none cannot be given one, so a change
  cannot both remove it and declare a total. Land the replacement first
  with no total declared, then declare budgets and the total in a following
  change, once the destination holds only the gates it keeps; the refusal
  names this route.

  `arc gates init [--runs N] [--publication]` measures six serial rounds of
  every gate that runs at integration, in any profile, by default. A
  publication-only gate is skipped and named unless `--publication` asks for
  it. A budget is worst run_ms plus spread (max minus min), at least 105% of
  worst, rounded up to two significant digits (minimum 1ms). Total is the
  sum of the integration gates' budgets, with no batch discount, and leaves
  publication-only budgets out. With no integration gate it refuses. Every other byte of .arc/gates.toml is kept. Operator-only
  gates are copied into the project. Existing budgets, failed or unmeasured
  rounds, or declaration edits during measurement refuse without writing.
  `arc gates over [CHANGE] [--gate G]` shows the newest measured run exceeding
  the applicable budget, its history sources, host observations and output
  artifact, cost kinds and slowest recorded checks, followed by the order of
  work in `arc guide gate-rounds`.

  `arc gates measure --concurrent` accepts a batch under `arc guide gates-together`.
  `arc gates measure [--runs N] [--gate G]` runs declarations sequentially on
  this tree, five times by default, honoring timeouts. Each round follows the
  `exclusive` and `admission` contract in `arc guide gates`. It prints command
  wall and tree CPU time, instructions and other processes' CPU share, with
  admission wait separately, the wall min, max and spread, and physical and
  logical host cores. Missing topology is unmeasured.
  Wall far above CPU indicates waiting inside the command; a wide spread
  indicates load sensitivity. A round not admitted prints
  `not measured (not-admitted)` and contributes no timing sample. Summaries
  count measured and unmeasured rounds. It records no evidence and writes no
  ledger or output artifacts; the gate commands may write their own build
  outputs. A failed, timed-out or unmeasured round makes the command exit 1.
  Unknown gates and zero runs are refused. Every declared gate runs unless
  `--gate` selects one.

  Five cost kinds:
  - waiting: paired wall/CPU medians have wall >= 100ms and wall >= twice CPU.
    CPU is observed child user plus system time; I/O and sleeps can both wait.
  - queueing: median wait_ms >= 100ms and >= 10% of median command wall time.
  - duplication: executions on integrated changes divided by integrated changes
    exceed one. The numerator matches the current command and timeout across
    environments; the denominator is every readable integrated change in the
    ledger, including changes answered entirely by reuse. Reuse, selection,
    base passes, attestation and not-admitted attempts contribute no executions.
    Changes without an execution still count in the denominator; no integrations is
    unmeasured, not a zero-cost claim.
  - preparation: median prepare_ms >= 100ms and >= 10% of median command wall.
  - real-work: paired wall/CPU measurements exist and the waiting threshold
    is not met. This is an indicator of command work, not proof of useful work.
  Waiting/real-work needs paired fields; queueing and preparation need their
  own field and wall. Absence never means zero.

  The order of work that `arc gates over` prints is steps 1 to 8 of a round
  in `arc guide gate-rounds`, which also holds the rules a gate follows.

  Levers, in order, are guidance arc states and does not automate:
  1. Use an optimized binary for spawned tests.
  2. Build an artifact the checks share once, behind a lock.
  3. Skip durability syscalls behind an explicit test-only switch.
  4. Use cheaper fixtures.
  5. Kill a throwaway process; do not ask it to exit.
  6. Use events in place of sleeps and polls.
  7. Increase harness parallelism or remove a shared serialization point.
  8. Call in process where no process is needed.
  9. Split a suite into gates by area with `paths`.
  10. Cut tests on evidence of what each has ever caught, ranked by what it guards.
  Cost and failures inform review; they do not choose which check to remove.

  A gate may declare timings = "checks.tsv". Its command writes the relative
  file inside its worktree on each execution. Separate gates use separate files.
  Arc never deletes it. A file unchanged since the attempt began is stale.
  The file must be regular, with no final symlink and no parent outside the
  worktree. Ignore generated timings in Git to keep execution evidence clean.
  Missing or inaccessible files, stale files, and malformed or oversized input
  produce an advisory receipt error, never a gate failure.

  The UTF-8 line format is `arc-check-timings/1` on the first line, followed
  by one check per line: name<TAB>milliseconds<TAB>pass|fail. Names are unique,
  nonempty, at most 128 UTF-8 bytes, and contain no control characters.
  Milliseconds are unsigned whole decimal integers. At least one check is
  required. Outcomes describe observations; the command's exit status decides
  the gate outcome. For example, with literal tab characters:

    arc-check-timings/1
    parse-config	12	pass
    reject-corruption	230	fail

  Input is bounded to 65,536 bytes and 512 check entries. An exceeded limit or
  any malformed entry discards the whole file with an error. Each run keeps
  at most 32 checks, failures first, then slowest within each outcome, with
  name as the tie break. Its receipt names the number dropped. These optional
  check_timings fields travel with command timing through replay and reuse;
  absence stays absent and older receipts remain valid. The path stays outside
  declaration identity and declaration-conflict checks: it observes a command
  and changes nothing the command checks or what its existing evidence proves.

  Reports aggregate direct executions of the current command and timeout in
  the newest environment. They show the ten slowest retained check names by
  median cost, participation count, and changes on which the check failed.
  A check with no retained failure prints "never failed in N runs, costs M
  seconds". N counts only runs retaining that check, not reuses or missing
  observations. This is recorded history, not proof that a check caught nothing
  or that it can be cut. Dropped checks and invalid or absent timings are
  counted explicitly. Arc builds no result store, impact planner or diff judge.
