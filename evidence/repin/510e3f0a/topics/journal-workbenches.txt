Workbenches for working material

WORKBENCHES (working material in the journal)
  A workbench is a directory under the journal's `workbench/` holding an
  index arc writes, `WORKBENCH.md`, and anything else: lane indexes and
  reports, drafts, raw measurements, a notebook appended to across sessions.
  Arc records the acts on it and never its content, which it reads only to
  count files and bytes and to see when anything in it last changed. Which
  material belongs in one is the rule in the map's KEEP WHAT YOU LEARN.
    arc journal workbench <slug> --purpose TEXT
                         Open `workbench/<slug>-<YYYYMMDD>/` and print its
                         absolute path as the last line, so a caller can
                         `cd` to it. Opening a slug already open with the
                         same purpose prints that workbench's path and
                         records nothing; another purpose refuses.
    arc journal workbench link <name> (--change C | --artifact FILE)
                         Record a change or an artifact the material serves.
    arc journal workbench close <name> --produced C|FILE... [--note TEXT]
                         Record what it produced: changes begun, artifacts
                         filed. One that produced nothing says why with
                         `--note`.
    arc journal workbench adopt PATH --purpose TEXT [--slug SLUG]
                         Move an existing directory, such as an entry of a
                         journal's `loose/`, or a file, such as a notebook
                         kept outside any journal, into a workbench by one
                         rename, which keeps every byte. Its help names what
                         it refuses.
    arc journal workbench list [--all] [--json]
                         Open workbenches, or all of them with what each
                         produced (`arc-journal-workbenches/1`).
    arc journal archive <name>, arc journal unarchive <name>
                         Move a closed workbench to the cold archive, and
                         back.
  A subcommand names a workbench by its name, or by its slug while one
  workbench of that slug is open. `catchup`, `journal catchup`, `journal
  open` and `workspace backlog` list each open workbench with its purpose,
  size and age, measured as `journal workbench list --help` says, and
  `workspace report` gives them a section. One open 14 days with no recorded
  act and no file in it changed is stale: the listings mark it, `catchup`
  and `workspace backlog` count it, and the report raises `stale-workbench`,
  as `journal filed` marks open items untouched that long.
  Journal exchange and its bundles carry no workbench: not its directory, its
  index, or its events. An artifact a workbench links or produced travels as
  any artifact does, without the link, and the workbench stays with the
  journal that holds it. Workbench commands need the journal directory
  itself and never spool.
  Opening, adopting, archiving and unarchiving create or move the directory,
  only through real directories of the journal or its archive, never a
  link, writing a new index only where no file of that name exists; then
  they record the act. Once the directory is made or moved, nothing undoes
  it, and the act ends one of two ways:
    recorded              The act stands.
    moved, not recorded   The material stays where it now is. The command
                          exits non-zero naming that path, `journal doctor`
                          reports the disagreement, and adopting the
                          directory where it stands, or rerunning the
                          archive or unarchive, records it.
  An open says `created, not recorded`, and an act that moved nothing says
  `nothing moved, not recorded`. When a `WORKBENCH.md` arc did not write
  stands in the directory, one made meanwhile or an index arc began and
  never finished, which lacks arc's marker, the message names it, and arc
  never overwrites, moves or renames it: move or rename it yourself, then
  adopt the directory where it stands. After every move arc confirms that
  what arrived is what it checked and that the directory it moved into is
  still the journal's; otherwise it stops, names where the material went,
  and records nothing. These guards stop mistakes, links, repositories,
  journals, another arc writer and files appearing while arc works; they
  do not defeat a process that deliberately swaps directory entries during
  the call, which no rename here can prevent. A record prepared but not yet published is
  the second until the next journal write publishes it. A later act rewrites the index only while it
  is the one arc wrote, and leaves any other `WORKBENCH.md` as it is. The
  rewrite is written beside it, under a name beginning
  `.WORKBENCH.md.arc-draft` that no other attempt uses, readable by its
  owner alone until written, then given exactly the index's mode and access
  ACL, none when it has none, and renamed over it, so a reader finds the old
  index or the new one whole; other extended attributes are not kept. A
  later rewrite removes the drafts interrupted ones left, which are empty or
  begin with part of arc's marker, and leaves any other file of those names
  alone. Arc reads a file in a workbench only as a regular file, opened
  without waiting, so a pipe or a device there never holds it.
  Adopting, closing, archiving and unarchiving refuse before reading or
  moving anything where `/dev/fd` or `/proc/self/fd` is missing, through
  which arc reads and names the directories it opens; a listing then gives
  each open workbench's size as unknown, naming the missing one.
  `journal doctor` reports a recorded workbench whose directory is gone, in
  the other storage, or reached through a link out of the journal, a
  `workbench/` or an archive root that is a link, and a directory under
  `workbench/` that no event records; `adopt` records one where it stands.
